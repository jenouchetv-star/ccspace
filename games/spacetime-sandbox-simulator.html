Einsteinian Mechanics

Einstein's General Relativity equation models gravitational coordinates curving directly in all directions:

$$G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}$$

Massive bodies curve the volumetric filaments of spacetime. Objects do not experience a "pull", but are instead following the shortest paths (geodesics) through curved 3D coordinates.

Cosmic Pilot Manual

1. Swirling 3D Spacetime Grid

Space is not static! In this upgraded physics model, spacetime is visualized as a lightweight, delicate three-dimensional filament web. As planets move, they exert a **frame-dragging force** that dynamically pulls, twists, and swirls the fabric of space-time around them in elegant gravitational vortex spirals.

2. Slicing Inside Core View (New!)

Select any planet and click "Slice Inside View". The celestial body will be sliced open along a $270^\circ$ section, revealing nested, chemically distinct solid and liquid layers (crust, mantle, and cores) rotating dynamically in real time.

3. Navigation Controls

  • Orbit Camera: Left-click and drag anywhere on the sky background.
  • Pan View: Right-click and drag (or hold Shift + Left drag).
  • Zoom: Use the scroll wheel to approach planets or inspect the solar system.
  • Quick Travel: Click names in the sidebar list to anchor camera tracking directly to that body.

4. Reposition Mode (Interactive Physics)

  1. Toggle interaction mode to "Warp Orbits (Drag)".
  2. Click and drag any planet in space to warp its distance from the central star.
  3. Release to launch the planet in its new circularized trajectory!
  4. Observe how other planets react to gravity changes or reset if they go chaotic!
Educational Insight: Press "Scaled Sizes" to compare Jupiter to Mercury. We've scaled planet sizes by orders of magnitudes to let you view both details and colossal cosmic scales simultaneously.
