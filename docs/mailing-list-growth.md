# Growing the Chike's Creative Space mailing list: a marketing strategy

The goal is not "more emails." It is a list of engaged parents and educators who open, click, come back to the
site, and eventually buy printables and books. Everything below is built around that.

Ground rule for all of it: never message current subscribers without a reason they signed up for, and never test
with their addresses.

---

## 1. Positioning: why someone joins

People don't join a newsletter. They join for a promise. Ours:

> **"One screen-free creative project a week your child will actually finish, plus the exclusive games and AI
> lessons only subscribers get."**

- **Who it's for:** parents of 4 to 10 year olds who worry about screen time, and teachers and homeschoolers who
  need ready-to-run hands-on lessons.
- **Why us:** the projects are tied to an animated series kids already watch, so the child asks to do them. Very few
  competitors connect episode, activity and game like this. That is the hook to repeat everywhere.
- **Proof:** the article research we cite (family involvement, creativity and confidence) and the real counts on the
  site (activities, games, episodes). Add parent and teacher quotes as soon as we have them.

Every sign-up box, ad and post repeats the same promise in the same words.

## 2. Segment from the first click

One list, three audiences with different reasons to stay:

| Segment | What they want | Signal at sign-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parents | Weekend and after-school projects, less screen time | Joined from an activity, game, episode or the Starter Pack |
| Teachers | Classroom-ready lessons, printables, standards links | Joined from the Classroom Pack or a teacher page |
| Homeschool families | Structured weekly plans, multi-age projects | Joined from the Grown-Up Guide or a homeschool group link |

Record which page and offer each person signed up from (the join form can store it). That lets us send each group
what it came for, and it tells us which offers work.

## 3. Lead magnets: one per segment and moment

Different offers convert different people. Build a small set and test them against each other:

- **Make It Real Starter Pack** (parents): printable activity cards plus a first-week plan. Download requires joining.
- **The Grown-Up Guide** (parents, homeschool): how to set up, what to say when it goes wrong, how to keep the
  learning going.
- **Classroom Pack** (teachers): a 4-week unit built from existing activities, with objectives and a materials
  list per lesson. Don't build six separate packs for K–5. Build one pack with three grade bands (K–1, 2–3, 4–5):
  the same activities, each with a band-specific goal, vocabulary and extension. Launch with one band (2–3 is the
  widest fit) and add the others once teachers respond. The sign-up form asks for the grade band, so each teacher
  gets their version and we learn which band to build next.
- **"Join free to play"** (kids and parents): the subscribers-only games and AI lessons we already have.
- **Episode companion sheets**: each new episode gets a one-page "make what you watched" printable, unlocked by email.

Rule: every offer gives something that isn't freely on the site (see "No repeated stories").

## 4. The funnel: where people meet us, and where we ask

**Attract** (get people to the site):
- **YouTube is the biggest lever.** In every episode, add a spoken or on-screen call to action ("Make the volcano
  from this episode: link below"). Link to that activity, not the home page. Add end screens, a pinned comment, and
  the link in the channel banner.
- **Pinterest.** Parents and teachers search it for kids' crafts and lesson ideas. Pin every activity with a tall
  image and a step preview, linking back to the activity page. Pins keep bringing traffic for months.
- **Search.** Write activity pages and articles around what parents actually type ("screen-free activities for
  7 year olds", "rainy day STEM projects at home"). The sitemap and page titles are already in place.
- **Short video.** Cut 15 to 30 second clips from episodes and activity how-tos for YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels
  and TikTok, each ending on the free offer.

**Convert** (turn a visit into a sign-up):
- Ask at the moment of success: a sign-up box at the end of every activity, beside every printable button, under
  finder results, and partway down each article.
- Match the offer to the page: an activity page offers "a new project like this every week," a game page offers
  "join free to play," an article offers the Grown-Up Guide.
- Keep the form short: one email field, the grown-up tick box, one sentence on what they get.

**Keep** (make them glad they joined):
- A 5-email welcome series after the confirm click:
  1. the promised download;
  2. the story of Chike and friends;
  3. the best activity for their child's age;
  4. the exclusive games;
  5. "reply and tell us your child's age".
- Then a predictable weekly email: one activity, one episode, one tip for grown-ups. Same day each week.

**Spread** (turn subscribers into recruiters):
- A referral reward: "Share your link; when 3 friends join, you unlock a bonus pack or an early episode."
- A "forward to a friend" line in every email, and share buttons on every activity (already there).

## 5. Partnerships: get members in batches

- **Schools and PTAs:** offer the Classroom Pack free to PTA newsletters in exchange for a mention.
- **Libraries:** pitch a "Make It Real" story-time kit (episode, book and activity) for children's librarians.
  Library events reach many families at once.
- **Homeschool co-ops and parenting groups** (Facebook groups, local co-ops): share the Grown-Up Guide, with the
  admins' permission.
- **Kids' creators and parenting bloggers:** trade guest activities or episode features. Their audience is ours.
- **Local businesses:** bookstores, craft stores, pediatric offices and children's museums can put a flyer with a QR
  code by the register.

## 6. Offline reach

- A QR code and "More free activities: scan to join" on every printable. They get handed around classrooms.
- Run workshops at libraries and schools, and collect sign-ups on a tablet at the door (grown-ups only, with consent).
- Put stickers or bookmarks with a QR code inside the Amazon books.

## 7. Paid acquisition, when the funnel is proven

Don't buy traffic until the free funnel converts. Then:
- Run Meta and Pinterest ads to the best lead magnet, targeting parents of young children and teachers.
- Start small, measure cost per confirmed subscriber, and only scale offers that pay back through printable or book
  sales.
- Retarget site visitors who didn't join with the offer for the page they read.

## 8. Timing

- **Back to school** (Aug–Sep): Classroom Pack push to teachers.
- **Winter break and summer:** "Screen-free break" project calendars for parents.
- **Holidays:** a gift guide built from our books and supplies.
- **Each new episode:** companion sheet, Shorts clips, and a weekly email feature.

## 9. Metrics: what to watch

| Metric | Why it matters | Healthy target to start |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitor-to-sign-up rate (by page) | Which pages and offers convert | 2–5% on activity pages |
| Confirm rate (double opt-in) | Real, reachable subscribers | 70%+ |
| Open and click rate | Whether they want what we send | 40%+ opens, 5%+ clicks |
| Sign-ups by source | Where to spend effort | Track weekly |
| Unsubscribe rate | Whether we send too much or the wrong thing | Under 0.5% per email |
| Subscriber-to-buyer rate | Whether the list pays for itself | Track once printables sell |

Run one experiment at a time (an offer, a headline, a box position) and keep the winner.

## 10. A 90-day plan

**Days 1–30: build the engine**
1. Finish the Starter Pack and Grown-Up Guide as editable pages with a sign-up-gated PDF download.
2. Add the matched sign-up box to activity, game, finder and article pages, recording the source page.
3. Write the 5-email welcome series.
4. Add the activity link and spoken call to action to every YouTube episode description and pinned comment.

**Days 31–60: open the channels**
5. Pin every activity on Pinterest; post 2 or 3 Shorts or Reels a week, cut from episodes.
6. Build the Classroom Pack and reach 10 PTAs, 5 libraries and 5 homeschool groups.
7. Start the weekly email on a fixed day.

**Days 61–90: compound and test**
8. Launch the referral reward.
9. A/B test the top two lead magnets on activity pages.
10. If cost per confirmed subscriber is known and healthy, start a small paid test.
