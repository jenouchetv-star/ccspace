# Game names: a clearance checklist

This is a working list, not legal advice. A game's rules and mechanics are generally not protected, but its name, logo, art and
overall look and feel can be. Before a name goes live (and ideally before it goes on a cover), do the checks below and keep a dated
note of what was searched and what was found. An intellectual-property attorney should make the final call on anything marked
"look closer".

## How to check a name

1. **Exact and near matches.** Search the name, its spelling variants and how it sounds, in the US trademark database
   (USPTO Trademark Search), on Google, in the Apple App Store and Google Play, on Steam, itch.io and Roblox, and on YouTube.
2. **The right classes.** Video games and downloadable games are class 9, online (non-downloadable) games and entertainment are
   class 41, and board games and toys are class 28. A name that is free in one class can still be taken in another that
   customers would link to it.
3. **Who owns it and how hard they enforce it.** A big publisher with a registered mark is a very different risk from an
   abandoned or tiny one.
4. **Domains and handles.** Check the .com, and the handles on the social sites the footer links to.
5. **Write it down.** Date, what was searched, what turned up, and the decision.
6. **Then decide whether to file** for the names you want to keep. Filing is what gives you something to defend.

## Pathbuilder

The road-building game was first called Road to Home, then Roadcraft. Roadcraft was dropped because I believe a commercial game
called RoadCraft (from Saber Interactive, published by Focus Entertainment) came out in 2025. The owner chose **Pathbuilder**.

- **Look closer.** I believe "Pathbuilder" (for example Pathbuilder 2e) is also the name of a well-known character-builder app
  for a tabletop role-playing game. That is a different kind of product, but it is also software, so run the searches above
  and look at how it is registered before the name spreads. Also expect generic "path builder" tools in routing and mapping.
- If it turns out to be too close, other options: Roadmakers, Pave the Way, Route Home, Bridge and Byway, Homeward Roads,
  Lay the Road, Road Home Builders.

## Gravity 4

- **Kept.** Fairly safe as long as it stays away from the Hasbro name. The site never writes "Connect 4" or "Connect Four" (a
  test guards this). "Four in a row" is a generic idea. Keep "Gravity 4", the chip colours and the board art clearly its own.
- Search "Gravity 4" and "Gravity Four" in classes 9, 28 and 41, because "Gravity" is a popular word for games.
- If it ever needs to change: Drop 4, Four Fall, Chip Drop Four, Four Down, Stack and Line 4, Drop and Line, Slide 4 Chips.

## Bounce & Bank

This game used to carry a name too close to a famous TV game show's trademark, and its page was titled after that show. The owner
chose **Bounce & Bank**, from these ten options:

1. Piggy Bounce
2. **Bounce & Bank** (chosen)
3. Coin Cascade
4. Peg and Penny
5. Savings Drop
6. Nest Egg Drop
7. Pegboard Bank
8. Drop and Save
9. Ricochet Bank
10. Penny Peg Drop

Run the same searches on "Bounce & Bank" and "Bounce and Bank" (banks and finance products use "bank" names heavily, so look at
class 36 too). The in-game bank is now called Bounce & Bank, and its saved balance uses a new storage key, so players start
from the opening balance once.

## DropBlox

The old pieces were the seven classic falling-block shapes. They are now eight five-block shapes of its own (see
`games/drop-blox.html`), and the page no longer carries a Tetris-named element. The look (a dark board, glossy coloured blocks)
should stay clearly different from the famous game.

## Names that look fine on their own

Air Hockey Arcade, Memory Match, Maze Quest, Spelling Detective, Balloon Pop Math, UFO Target Practice and Solar System Sandbox are
descriptive or generic. Doodle Dash is a little close to a popular app with a similar first word, so give it the same search.
